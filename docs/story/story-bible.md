# Naija Street Life: Up NEPA! — Story Bible

*Story Mode deliverable 1 of 6. Draft v0.1, 2026-10-14. For owner approval.*

> **"When the light comes back, everybody will see what you did."**

⚠ **Language review:** every Pidgin, Yoruba, Igbo and Hausa word or line in this
document is **pending native-speaker review**. The terms are collected in the
glossary (§10). Nothing ships until each one is ticked.

---

## 1. Premise
At a lavish Lekki owambe, NEPA takes the light for two seconds. In that blackout,
the campaign war chest of **Chief Dr. Sir Barr. Olusegun "Double Chop"
Adebayo-Williams** vanishes. It's a Ghana-must-go bag stuffed with cash. He is
running for Local Government Chairman of the fictional **Agbado-Ketu Central LGA**.
When the light snaps back, the whole hall roars **"UP NEPA!"**, and the Chief's
men start looking for the bag, the phone that filmed everything, and everybody
seen in that video.

Three ordinary Lagosians are now tangled in the mess:
- a **danfo driver** whose bus the bag ends up in
- a **canteen queen and POS agent** the Chief owes money to
- her **nephew, a broke skit-maker**, whose livestream caught every face in the hall

Over one chaotic election season they argue, scheme, feed each other and pick up
fights across the city. In the end they decide what Agbado-Ketu Central deserves,
and what each of them is really hustling for.

## 2. Themes
| Theme | How it shows up |
|---|---|
| **Who controls the light** | Electricity and political power are the same joke. The Chief promises light; NEPA gives and takes it; the finale weaponises it. |
| **Hustle vs. community** | Every hero dreams alone: a fleet, an empire, japa. Each wins only by leaning on the others and on the street (market women, passengers, followers). |
| **Truth vs. gist** | KC can make anyone believe anything. The story asks what that power costs, especially in an election. |
| **Debt and obligation** | Everyone owes someone. Aunty Boss remembers the exact amount. The Chief owes the whole ward. |
| **Staying vs. leaving** | Japa is a real dream, not a punchline. The story respects both choices. |

**Comedy rules** (from the brief, restated as checks):
- Punch **up** and **sideways**: at big men, bureaucracy, hustle culture and self-deception.
- **Never** mock ethnic groups, religions, disabilities or poverty itself.
- Laughs come from **character**, **situation** and **everyday absurdity**: power
  cuts, go-slow, POS network failure, fuel queues, aso-ebi pressure,
  prosperity-gospel radio, relatives with a "small request".
- **Target rhythm:** a laugh every 2–3 minutes, and a sincere beat every mission.
- **Violence** is slapstick only: falling into gutters, flying ogogoro bottles,
  wrestling over a generator. No blood, no gore, no guns pointed at heroes on screen.
- **No crime tutorials:** hustlers can be scrappy, but the game never shows a
  working fraud method (see §8).

## 3. The Three Heroes

### 3.1 TUNDE "BABA GOD DEY" ADEYEMI, 34, danfo driver
**Look:** wiry, sun-dark, faded Lagos-United-style jersey (fictional club "Eko
Thunder FC"), towel on the shoulder, one chewing stick that never leaves his mouth.

**The bus:** a yellow danfo named **"Baba God Dey"** (painted on the back, with "No
Condition Is Permanent" on the side), held together by prayer, masking tape and
spite. The bus is basically the fourth hero. It has moods, a horn that sounds like
a goat, and a sliding door that only opens for people Tunde likes.

**Dream:** a fleet called **"Baba God Dey Express"**, with matching paint and
air-conditioning "that works".

**Personality:**
- Loud, fearless, and a philosopher of traffic ("Go-slow is God's way of telling
  you to think").
- Argues football and politics with every passenger.
- Allergic to road wardens.

**Flaw:** he can't resist a shortcut, even when it's on fire. *Especially* when
it's on fire.

**Voice:**
- Booming, Pidgin-first, with proverbs he half-remembers and finishes wrong.
- Calls everyone by job title ("Customer!", "Madam Lawyer!", "Oga Police!").
- Example: *"I no carry bag. I carry people. Bag na your own wahala."*

**Stat: Blood Pressure (BP)** 🩸
- **Goes up with:** go-slow, wardens, passengers who say "I no get change", and
  being overtaken by a keke.
- **High BP:** better ramming and horn-intimidation, but the camera shakes, steering
  gets twitchy and passengers get nervous.
- **Max BP:** a comic "Wahala Mode" tantrum. He gets out of the bus to argue and the
  timer keeps running.
- **Lowered by:** agege bread, Fuji on the radio, a good argument won, or Aunty
  Boss's pepper soup.

**Abilities:**
- **Go-Slow Whisperer:** time slows, and hidden bypass routes light up (through a
  market, behind a church, the "one-way" every driver uses).
- **Conductor Combo:** with his conductor **Sule**, chain calls ("Obalende! Enter
  with your change!") to talk passengers into anything: holding the door, ducking,
  lying to a warden, singing.

**Signature gag:** passengers keep arguing loudly in the back during action scenes,
and sometimes help or sabotage. In a chase, a prophetess in the back seat declares
the next turn "a turn of favour". It's a dead end.

**Music:** Fuji and street Afrobeats (original tracks only).

**Relationships:**
- **Aunty Boss:** they bicker like an old married couple who were never married.
  He eats at her canteen on credit and she lets him, which nobody understands.
- **KC:** owes Tunde **three months of bus fares** (Tunde keeps a notebook). Tunde
  calls him "Influenza".
- **Sule (conductor, 19):** quiet, hyper-competent, secretly the real brain of the bus.

**Secret:**
- Two years ago Tunde drove the Chief's campaign bus in the *last* election for
  ₦15,000 and a promise of "a whole fleet after we win".
- The Chief won, and the promise vanished.
- Tunde has never told anyone. He's ashamed he believed it.
- When he finds the bag, part of him thinks: *this is my fleet money.*

**Arc:** *Shortcut to the long road.*
- Starts wanting the quick win: the bag, the shortcut, the promise.
- Learns that the only fleet worth having is one the community trusts.
- In the Honest ending, his danfo becomes the **ward's official shuttle**; one bus,
  but it's *his*.

### 3.2 NGOZI "AUNTY BOSS" EZE, 41, canteen queen, POS agent, ajo organiser
**Look:**
- Short and solid.
- Ankara wrapper and gele in whatever colour wins the argument that day.
- Reading glasses on a chain, which she lowers to judge you.
- POS machine in a holster like a sheriff.

**Business:**
- A mama-put canteen, **"Aunty Boss Kitchen: Food Is Ready"**, with a POS point
  under a big umbrella next to it.
- She also runs an **ajo** (rotating savings circle) of 40 market women. Banks are
  afraid of it.

**Dream:** a catering empire, then a hotel with her name on it ("NGOZI SUITES:
Rest Is Ready").

**Personality:**
- Motherly and ruthless.
- She can end a man with one sentence and then feed him.
- Everybody owes her money, and she remembers the **exact** amount, including kobo.

**Flaw:** she can't forgive a debt. Not ₦200. Not during a heist. Not when the
building is on fire.

**Voice:**
- Calm, precise and terrifying.
- English when she's being polite, Pidgin when she's winning, Igbo when she's
  *finished* with you.
- Food metaphors for everything.
- Example: *"You see this bag? It is like stew. Everybody wants to put hand. Nobody
  wants to wash pot."*

**Stat: Aunty Aura** 👑
- **High Aura:** NPCs back down, haggle lower or confess.
- **Raised by:** feeding people, collecting debts, winning arguments, keeping her
  word.
- **Dropped by:** being disrespected in public, failing a promise to the ajo, or
  being seen to "beg".

**Abilities:**
- **The Stare:** a timed focus mechanic. NPCs freeze, confess, or hand over the
  thing. It works on henchmen, bouncers and children. It doesn't work on her own
  mother.
- **Ajo Network:** call in favours from market women across the city:
  - fast travel by "a trusted keke"
  - hideouts in stalls
  - free food (restores needs)
  - intel ("the Chief's driver bought suya at 9pm, he was sweating")

**Signature gag:** she negotiates with villains as if she's selling tomatoes. *"You
said ₦5 million? Bring it down. Bring it DOWN. Is it gold you're selling?"*

**Music:** highlife and gospel-tinged Afrobeats.

**Relationships:**
- **KC:** her late sister's son. She raised him from 12. She's proud of him and
  baffled by him, and she hides it under nagging.
- **Tunde:** her longest-running debtor and most loyal customer.
- **The Chief:** he owes her **₦2,340,500** for catering his last campaign's rallies.
  She has the receipts. She *always* has the receipts.

**Secret:**
- She recognises the Ghana-must-go bag the moment she sees it.
- *It's hers.* It's her old ajo bag, with her own stitching on the handle.
- She lent it to the Chief's wife years ago to carry food trays, and it was never
  returned.
- Someone has been using her bag to move his money. That's personal.

**Arc:** *The debt you can't collect.*
- She believes the world runs on ledgers.
- She learns that some debts, like those owed to a ward, a nephew or a dead sister,
  can't be settled in naira.
- In the Honest ending she forgives one debt publicly: Tunde's. It's the funniest
  and most moving beat in the game.

### 3.3 KELECHI "KC VIBES" NWOSU, 23, aspiring skit maker and influencer
**Look:** Island-style fit bought in Yaba, ring light in a backpack, a tripod like
a sword, and a phone with a cracked screen and a "battery case" that doesn't charge.

**Home:**
- A crowded Yaba room-and-parlour with three flatmates and one socket.
- Online, he pretends to live in Lekki (green-screen balcony).

**Dream:** go viral, get a brand deal, and **japa**. Every plan is a content plan.

**Personality:**
- Charming, anxious and always filming.
- Narrates his own life in skit voice.
- Genuinely brave at the worst possible moment.

**Flaw:** he can't resist a perfect shot, even while running for his life.

**Voice:**
- Fast, Gen-Z Lagos English mixed with Pidgin and internet slang.
- Talks to camera mid-crisis.
- Example: *"Guys. Guys. If I die today, the link is in my bio."*

**Stats: Hype 🔥 and Phone Battery 🔋**
- **Battery:**
  - Drains constantly, faster while filming or on Go Live.
  - Recharge points: NEPA (when it feels like it), generator owners (who charge
    ₦200 and a story), power banks (single-use), and Aunty's canteen socket ("Ask
    first!").
  - At 0%, KC can't use abilities and starts narrating to strangers instead.
- **Hype:**
  - Rises with good content and falls with flops.
  - **High Hype** opens doors (bouncers, influencer events, crowd support).

**Abilities:**
- **Go Live:** filming makes crowds react. They can rally behind a hero, turn on an
  enemy, or part like the Red Sea for a "content creator coming through".
- **Gist:** start a rumour that changes what NPCs believe about a **fictional
  character or event**. Example: "the Chief's driver is the real thief" makes
  henchmen fight each other.
  - Gist spreads through the NPC network: WhatsApp groups, market women, danfo
    passengers.
  - It can **backfire**: if it's false and gets debunked, KC's Hype crashes and
    people stop trusting him. This is the moral teeth of his arc.

**Signature gag:**
- Begs strangers to "like and subscribe".
- Loses signal at every dramatic moment. The screen freezes on his face mid-scream.
  "…guys?"

**Music:** Amapiano and street-pop.

**Relationships:**
- **Aunty Boss:** she's his whole world, and he'd rather die than say so on camera.
  He secretly films her cooking for a channel he's never posted: "Aunty Boss
  Kitchen TV".
- **Tunde:** owes him three months of fares. Calls him "Uncle Traffic". Secretly
  thinks he's the coolest man in Lagos.

**Secret:**
- KC has **already applied for a visa**.
- He paid the fee with money "borrowed" from Aunty's ajo box, and plans to replace
  it "after the brand deal".
- The interview is in **Act 2**.
- If Aunty finds out before he confesses, Aura and family trust collapse. That's a
  branching beat.

**Arc:** *Going viral vs. being real.*
- He learns the difference between attention and trust.
- Gist can move a crowd, but only the truth moves a ward.
- In the finale he goes live with **receipts**, not rumours.
- **Japa ending:** he leaves. It's sad and funny, and he's not a traitor; he
  posts "Aunty Boss Kitchen TV" from abroad and it blows up.

## 6. Plot outline
### Prologue: "Owambe, Owambe" (tutorial; play all three)
- **Tunde** drives guests in, then gets dragged inside by a guest who "knows the
  family". *Teaches:* driving and the BP meter.
- **Aunty Boss** caters (the jollof queue is politics). *Teaches:* serving, The
  Stare, and debt reminders.
- **KC** films for content. *Teaches:* camera, Battery and Hype.
- **Shared:**
  - The **money-spraying** mini-game (rhythm and timing).
  - An **aso-ebi dress-code check** at the door.
  - MC chaos: "the groom's mother will dance for the third time."
- **The blackout** (see the sample scene in the brief). The bag vanishes in a
  two-second flash.
- **"UP NEPA!"**, then the title card: *Naija Street Life: Up NEPA!*

### Act 1: "Light Out" (solo intros, then convergence)
1. **"Baba God Dey"** (Tunde): a day of passengers with strong opinions. One skips
   paying "because I no get change" and leaves the bag behind. Tunde finds it at
   the depot.
2. **"Ajo Day"** (Aunty Boss): collect contributions from stubborn debtors while the
   POS network keeps failing. She hears the Chief's men are hunting "a canteen
   woman" from the video.
3. **"Four Percent"** (KC): his livestream clip has 40,000 viewers, his phone is at
   4% and there's no light. A frantic hunt for power across Yaba. The Chief's men
   start following the clip.
4. **"Family Meeting"** (convergence): all three meet at the canteen, over generator
   noise, with the bag on the table.
   - Aunty recognises her stitching. Tunde hides his secret. KC wants to film it.
   - **First major choice:** hide the bag, return it, or "count it first".

### Act 2: "Spread" (solo arcs, co-op missions, escalating chaos)
Mission spine (full list in deliverable 2):
- **"Generator Wars":** a night run to move a generator and diesel past rival streets.
- **"Danfo Chase":** Pepper and Salt chase Tunde through go-slow while passengers
  **vote** on each turn.
- **"Japa Interview":** KC's visa interview with the Northvale officer. Outcome
  flags the Japa ending.
- **"Aso-Ebi Infiltration":** all three sneak into Mama Chief's 90th as guests, with
  strict dress codes and aunties asking "when are you marrying?".
- **"Following the Money"** *(reworks "Small Small Money", see §8)*: Aunty's market
  network traces **how Barrister Ledger has been splitting the Chief's cash through
  dozens of POS points**. The heroes collect *evidence* (receipts, CCTV, agent
  testimony); they don't launder anything.
- **"Fuel Queue Standoff":** a negotiation and slapstick brawl at a PetroNaija station.
- **"Viral":** KC's Gist and Go Live swing public opinion. The ambient gossip system
  reacts, including real-world-news chatter as flavour only.
- **Midpoint twist:**
  - The bag holds mostly **old, withdrawn notes**. The Chief has been hoarding
    worthless cash.
  - The real prize is **Barrister Ledger's diary**: receipts of every promise and
    payment.

### Act 3: "Election Day"
- The Agbado-Ketu Central election runs through the **poll and election system**.
- Rumours, real votes, bribes (they always backfire on the briber) and promises
  swing the result.
- **Finale: "Up NEPA!"**
  1. The heroes bribe Engineer Bayo with suya.
  2. They time a power restoration to the second the Chief takes the stage.
  3. Every light, speaker and phone comes on.
  4. KC goes live with the diary.
  5. Aunty Boss reads the receipts aloud, *to the kobo*.
  6. Tunde blocks the Chief's convoy exit with Baba God Dey.

### Endings (decided by choices across the acts)
| Ending | Condition (summary) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| **A: Honest** | Return the money, expose with evidence, keep family trust high | The money becomes a **transformer and a borehole** for the ward. The heroes stay broke but respected. Teacher wins. Tunde's bus becomes the ward shuttle. |
| **B: Hustle** | Keep the bag | Money and trouble multiply. The FCIB circles. Friendships crack, and the last scene is three people eating separately. |
| **C: Japa** | KC passes the interview and chooses to go | KC leaves. Tunde and Aunty's story continues in free roam. Sad-funny video-call epilogue. |
| **D: Secret** | Find all diary pages and open the bag with Aunty's stitch-key | The real prize is the **diary**. The money was mostly old notes. A twist epilogue sets up a sequel. |

## 7. Switching mechanic: "Family Meeting 🔥"
- Switch heroes from the in-game phone's group chat, **Family Meeting 🔥** (members:
  Aunty Boss, KC, Tunde, and Sule, who never types).
- **Each switch plays a 3–6 message chat exchange** (from a bank; see deliverable 5):
  - "Where are you?" / "Dey come." / "Which one is 'dey come'?"
- **Off-screen characters keep living** on a lightweight life-sim simulation. On
  return, there's a "while you were away" event:
  - The danfo was towed by LARDO.
  - Aunty Boss started a dispute with a pepper seller (it's now a court case).
  - KC went viral for the wrong reason: he was filmed falling into a gutter.
- **Rules:**
  - Switching is disabled mid-mission unless the mission is designed for it (co-op
    missions use hot-swap).
  - **Story-critical** beats are scripted. Ambient off-screen events come from a
    weighted table (rules-based; Gemini may only write the flavour text; see §9).

## 8. Content-safety rework notes
These are **proposed changes to the seed, for your approval**:
1. **"Small Small Money" → "Following the Money."**
   - The original has the heroes split cash across POS agents so the bank doesn't
     flag it. That is a step-by-step of **structuring**, a real money-laundering
     technique, and it breaks the "don't teach crime" rule.
   - **Rework:** the *villain's treasurer* did it, and the heroes uncover it through
     Aunty's agent network. The comedy stays (POS agents, network failure, nosy
     agents), and the plot points toward exposure.
2. **Bribery in Act 3.** The player *can* offer bribes, but they **always backfire**
   in funny ways (the recipient films it, or bribes the other side for more). They
   never reliably win votes.
3. **Gist (rumours)** only ever targets **fictional** characters and events. It is
   never wired to real-world news items or real people. False gist can be debunked
   and costs KC Hype.
4. **Japa Interview** uses the fictional Republic of Northvale. The joke is the
   bureaucracy, not any real country or its people.
5. **Prosperity radio.** The satire targets a con-man pastor character's
   *commercial* behaviour (selling anointed ringtones), never faith or worship.
   Church and mosque appear as warm community spaces.
6. **Old notes.** The "withdrawn notes" twist stays fictional and doesn't reference
   any real currency-redesign event.

## 9. World rules
1. **The light cycle.**
   - Every district has power-state cycles from Light & Darkness.
   - Outages change gameplay: shops close, generators roar and drown out dialogue,
     KC's charging options vanish, mosquitoes attack Tunde's BP.
2. **Time.** Story missions pause the life-sim clock. Free roam runs it.
3. **The city remembers.** NPC opinion of each hero (Respect / Fear / Gist) persists,
   and Gist spreads along the social graph.
4. **Scripted vs. AI:**
   - **Story-critical** NPCs and beats are 100% scripted.
   - AI (rules plus Gemini, per D-003) handles only **ambient** behaviour and chatter: what a passenger argues about, whether
     a market woman haggles, which side an NPC votes for in side elections.
   - The plot can't break.
5. **News is flavour, never plot.** Radio, WhatsApp gist and NPC banter can react
   to real headlines, through guardrails. No story scene depends on them, and the
   feature can be switched off.
6. **No real people.** No real politician, celebrity or public figure is named,
   depicted or quoted. Real-world news is referred to as headline topics with
   source credit, never as invented quotes.
7. **Money.** Story money is separate from free-roam money until the story ends, so
   the endings can't be farmed.
8. **Failure is funny.** Every mission failure has a comedic fail line and a quick retry.

## 11. How the story connects to the other systems (summary; full spec is deliverable 6)
| System | Story use |
|---|---|
